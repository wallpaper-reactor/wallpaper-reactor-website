**Windows 11 can't do this on its own.** Personalization gives you a still image, or a slideshow that swaps between still images, and that's where it stops. There's no setting to turn on. You need an app that draws behind your icons for you.

Here's the whole thing, start to finish.

## 1. Install Wallpaper Reactor

It's a [free download](/releases/), and it's in the Microsoft Store. You don't need an account for any of this — the library and everything below work signed out.

## 2. Open Browse and pick something

The **Browse** tab is your library: hundreds of wallpapers sorted into Recently Added, Hidden Gems, Most Downloaded and Most Favorited. Scroll until something catches your eye, then click it.

## 3. Download it

Selecting a wallpaper fills the panel on the right — preview, who made it, size, and a **Download** button. Click that first. This is the step people miss: until the file is on your PC, there's nothing to set.

## 4. Set it

Once it's downloaded, the same button becomes **Set**. Click it, and it's live on your desktop.

That's it. Four clicks and your desktop is moving.

## Worth knowing

**More than one monitor?** You choose which screen a wallpaper goes on, so each display can run something different.

**Not all live wallpapers are big files.** The one above is a shader — code that draws itself, rather than stored video — and it's 9.66 KB. Smaller than most photos you've taken.

**It gets out of the way.** Wallpapers pause rather than compete with whatever you're doing, so a game or a fullscreen video isn't sharing your GPU with your desktop background.

**Same library elsewhere.** The steps are near enough identical [on a Mac](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-mac/) and [on Android](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-android/).

## Want your own video instead?

You can use a clip you already have — an MP4, a WebM, even a GIF — and it takes about fifteen seconds with no account. That's a short walkthrough of its own: [how to set any video as a live wallpaper](/blog/set-any-video-as-live-wallpaper/).
