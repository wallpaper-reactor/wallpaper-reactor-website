Live wallpapers have a reputation for being something only new phones can afford to run. That reputation is mostly out of date, and it goes back further than people expect: Wallpaper Reactor installs on **Android 9**, which shipped in 2018. If your phone still gets apps from the Play Store, it can almost certainly run this.

## Why older phones struggle

It helps to know what's actually expensive, because it isn't the wallpaper being "heavy" in any general sense. It's pixels per second — how many dots the phone has to draw, multiplied by how many times a second it draws them.

A 4K clip at 60 frames per second asks for about sixteen times the work of a 1080p clip at 30. Same wallpaper, same idea, wildly different cost. Everything below follows from that one fact.

## What to pick

**Short video loops.** A ten to twenty second clip at 1080p is the safest thing you can put on an older phone. It's a format the hardware has a dedicated decoder for, which is exactly why it's cheap.

**Rive animations.** Wallpaper Reactor runs these as wallpapers, and they're vector animations rather than video — they're drawn from shapes instead of stored as pixels. Files are usually under a megabyte, they stay sharp at any screen size, and they're inexpensive to draw. On a phone that's tight on both storage and power, they're often the best of both.

**Go carefully with heavy shaders and 4K video.** Not never — just not the first thing to try. A shader that does a lot of maths for every pixel is the one category where an older GPU will genuinely feel it.

## Turn the cost down

You don't have to guess at any of this. Wallpaper Reactor's performance modes set the frame rate and how much of your screen's resolution a wallpaper actually renders at:

| Mode | Frame rate | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Battery Saver | 15 fps | 50% |
| Balanced | 30 fps | 75% |
| High FPS | native | 75% |
| High Resolution | 30 fps | 100% |
| High Quality | 60 fps | 90% |
| Max Quality | native | 100% |

Battery Saver asks for roughly an eighth of the work Max Quality does. On a phone that's showing its age, that's the difference between a wallpaper you keep and one you turn off after a day.

Balanced is the sensible middle, and honestly most people can't tell 30 from 60 on a wallpaper they aren't staring at.

## What Wallpaper Reactor does on its own

Two things happen without you setting anything up.

Wallpaper Reactor **pauses when your phone enters battery saver** — it watches for the system switching that on, and stops drawing rather than competing with the thing trying to save your battery. And it has a separate control for whether wallpapers run on battery at all, if you'd rather they only animate while charging.

Both are on by default, so the sensible behaviour is what you get out of the box.

## An old phone is a fine place for this

There's a decent argument that a hand-me-down phone is a *better* candidate for a live wallpaper than a new one — it's the device most likely to be sitting on a desk being looked at rather than carried around.

It's free, it runs on Android 9 and up, and when you do upgrade, the same library comes with you.
