macOS is a near miss here. It ships **dynamic** wallpapers — the desert and beach ones that shift from morning to night — and it'll play a video as a screen saver. What it won't do is put a moving wallpaper on your desktop while you're using it. For that you need an app.

Here's the whole thing.

## 1. Install Wallpaper Reactor

Grab the [free download for macOS](/releases/). Take the direct download rather than the App Store build — the store version is a cut-down "Lite" release, and the direct one is the full app.

No account needed for anything below.

## 2. Open Browse and pick something

The **Browse** tab holds the library: hundreds of wallpapers grouped into Recently Added, Hidden Gems, Most Downloaded and Most Favorited. Scroll, then click one.

## 3. Download it

Selecting a wallpaper fills the panel on the right — preview, creator, file size, and a **Download** button. Click that first. Until the file is on your Mac there's nothing to set, which is where people get stuck looking for a button that hasn't appeared yet.

## 4. Set it

Once it's downloaded, that same button becomes **Set**. Click it and your desktop is moving.

## Worth knowing

**On a MacBook, battery matters.** Wallpapers pause under battery saver and there are power-saving modes for when you're unplugged, so this isn't a constant tax on your afternoon.

**Live wallpapers aren't necessarily heavy.** The one above is a shader — code that draws itself rather than a stored video — at 9.66 KB.

**More than one display?** Each screen can run its own wallpaper.

**Same library elsewhere.** The steps are near enough identical [on Windows 11](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-windows-11/) and [on Android](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-android/).

## Want your own video instead?

Any clip you already have works — MP4, WebM, MOV, even a GIF — in about fifteen seconds, with no account. That's its own short walkthrough: [how to set any video as a live wallpaper](/blog/set-any-video-as-live-wallpaper/).
