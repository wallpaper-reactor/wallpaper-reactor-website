## Why bother on a handheld like this

The top screen is the reason you bought a two-screen device, and most of the time it's sitting there doing nothing. A live wallpaper gives it something to do — and on a machine built to look like a DS, that's half the appeal.

It also looks far better than it has any right to on a small screen. Motion reads well at that size.

## Setting one up

Four taps, and you don't need an account for any of it.

1. **Install Wallpaper Reactor.** It's a [free download](/releases/), and it runs on Android 9 and up, which every one of these handhelds clears comfortably.
2. **Open Browse** in the bottom bar and scroll the library.
3. **Tap a wallpaper, then Download it.** The button only becomes *Set* once the file is on the device.
4. **Tap Set.**

If you want it spelled out with screenshots, the [Android walkthrough](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-android/) covers the same four steps in more detail.

## A different wallpaper on each screen

Out of the box, both screens get the same wallpaper — that's what the clip above is doing. Giving them different ones takes one setting.

1. Open **Settings** from the bottom bar.
2. Scroll down to **Multi-Monitor Mode**.
3. Change it from **Duplicate** to **Custom**.

Choose **Monitor 1** for one wallpaper, then go back, pick another, and choose **Monitor 2**. A calm one up top and something busier below. A clock on one and artwork on the other. Whatever suits each screen.

## Keeping it easy on the battery

These are handhelds, so it's worth a thought. Wallpapers pause when the device drops into battery saver, and there's a separate switch for whether they run on battery at all.

If you want to spend even less, performance modes cap the frame rate and the resolution a wallpaper renders at — the same trick that makes this work nicely on [older or cheaper Android hardware](/blog/live-wallpapers-old-android-phone/).

## Worth a try

It's free, it takes about a minute, and if you don't like it you set a normal wallpaper back. For a device with a screen that usually sits idle, it's the cheapest upgrade available.
