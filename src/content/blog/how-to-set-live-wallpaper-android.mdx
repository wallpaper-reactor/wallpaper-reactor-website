Good news: unlike Windows and macOS, **Android has supported live wallpapers since 2010**. It's built into the system. What you need is an app that gives you good ones and a sane way to set them.

Here's the whole thing, in four taps.

## 1. Install Wallpaper Reactor

It's a [free download](/releases/) on the Play Store, and it runs on Android 9 and up — so a phone from 2018 is still fine. No account needed for any of this.

## 2. Open Browse and tap a wallpaper

**Browse** is in the bottom bar. The library is grouped into Recently Added, Hidden Gems, Most Downloaded and Most Favorited — scroll until something appeals, then tap it.

## 3. Download it

Tapping a wallpaper opens its own screen: a big preview, who made it, and a **Download** button. Tap that first — until the file is on your phone there's nothing to set.

Once downloaded, that button becomes **Set**. Tap it and it's your wallpaper.

## Worth knowing

**Battery is handled for you.** Wallpapers pause when your phone switches into battery saver, and there's a separate control for whether they run on battery at all.

**Older phone?** Performance modes let you cap the frame rate and the resolution a wallpaper renders at, which makes a real difference on hardware that's a few years old — that's covered properly in [live wallpapers on an old or cheap Android phone](/blog/live-wallpapers-old-android-phone/).

**On a computer too.** The same library and the same four steps work [on Windows 11](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-windows-11/) and [on a Mac](/blog/how-to-set-live-wallpaper-mac/).

**Plugging into a monitor?** Desktop Mode is the one place this doesn't work yet, and [that's Android's doing rather than the app's](/blog/android-desktop-mode-live-wallpapers/).
